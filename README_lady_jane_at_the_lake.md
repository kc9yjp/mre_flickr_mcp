## The Critique

*Filed from a desk somewhere west of Lake Michigan.*

Twelve thousand nine hundred forty-two views — the most-seen frame in this entire top
five, sunrise included — and nine favorites. That ratio is a story about how the internet
finds pictures, not about this picture. So let's talk about the picture.

What's working here is wind. This is Silver Lake, Wisconsin, Memorial Day weekend, and
anybody who has stood on a Midwest dock in late May knows that exact breeze. It lifts the
red hair off her shoulders and puts actual weather into what would otherwise be a
stand-still-and-smile, and the smile itself is the genuine article — nobody manufactures
that one on request. Red hair against spring-green trees is the kind of complementary
color scheme people pay money to fake. And look close at the sunglasses: the photographer
is reflected in both lenses, a little self-portrait smuggled into the frame. That's the
detail you find on the third look, and third looks are what photographs are for.

What holds it back is noon. Straight-up midday light is nobody's friend — flat,
bright-overcast, nothing to model the face. The sunglasses take her eyes, which means the
smile has to carry the entire portrait alone, and it very nearly does. Meanwhile the big
lake house over her shoulder keeps auditioning for second billing that nobody offered it.
Ten feet to the left, open shade, glasses pushed up on the forehead — that's a keeper
portrait. This is a good one.

**Verdict:** The warmest picture of the five, and around here warmth counts for plenty.
But the eyes are the whole ballgame in a portrait this tight, and the ballgame got
played behind smoked glass.
