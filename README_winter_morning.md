## The Critique

*Filed from a desk somewhere west of Lake Michigan.*

Do the arithmetic on this one, because the numbers are telling a story. Uploaded the
first of December; 206 favorites by mid-July — call it 27 a month. Nothing else in the
top five is gathering faves at even half that pace; the mighty Sky and Cosmos averaged
nine. On fave *rate* — 206 faves against barely three thousand views — this is, quietly,
the most persuasive photograph on the account: almost seven percent of everyone who saw
it loved it. The crowd is seldom unanimous. This is what unanimous looks like.

And the crowd's instinct is correct, because this is the picture with *time* in it. The
street lamp still burning in the upper left tells you the day hasn't officially started.
The pink — that magenta-to-salmon fire the description rightly calls a brief window — is
the ten-minute kind; you either had your boots on or you missed it. The snow holds the
sky's pink on one side and its own blue on the other. Somebody already shoveled the walk.
December dawn in a Midwest neighborhood, first of the month, 6:47 AM: the phone is
straining — 1/40th of a second, ISO 800, and yes, you can find the grain in the shadows
if you go looking. Don't go looking.

Quibbles, honestly held: the big trunk right of center stands squarely in front of the
best of the sky like a tall man arriving late to the movies. The frame is an inventory
more than a composition — fence, pole, wires, chain-link, garage — with no single
resting place for the eye. A step left and lower might have let the lamp and the pink
own the picture outright.

But the clutter is also the truth. This is what a neighborhood dawn actually is —
cluttered, cold, briefly outrageous overhead, and over by seven. The lamp alone earns
the fave.

**Verdict:** The newest picture in the five and the fastest-loved thing the account has
ever produced. If the trend line holds, next winter this is the most-faved photo on the
account — and I won't be filing an objection.
