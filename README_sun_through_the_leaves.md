## The Critique

*Filed from a desk somewhere west of Lake Michigan.*

The description opens with a confession — "It's hard to resist this shot when you know
your iPhone can do it" — and honestly, that's the review. The photographer knew exactly
what this was when he took it. My job is just to co-sign with a few particulars.

What the iPhone did, it did well: rust and amber oak leaves backlit to stained glass,
every vein rendered like the leading in a chapel window, and behind them the right
background — bare November trees dissolved into cool steel-blue bokeh, the cold blue foil
that makes the warm glass burn. Late autumn's whole argument in two temperatures. The
picture's best secret is the shadow play: look at the big leaf left of center carrying
the silhouette of *another* leaf cast onto it by the sun — a photograph happening inside
the photograph. That detail is the reward for the third look, and third looks are what
photographs are for.

Now the other hand. This is, structurally, the calendar shot. October and November
perform this show every single year, free of charge, and every camera club from Rockford
to Kankakee has a wall of it. The frame is an allover pattern with no lead actor — no
single leaf outranks the others, so the eye circles the frame without ever landing. And
the soft edges read processed — that smeared blur at the corners feels applied rather
than optical, a little velvet rope around a picture that didn't need one.

**Verdict:** 211 people faved it because it's beautiful, and it is. I'd only note that
*beautiful* and *memorable* are different words. This is the most replaceable picture in
the top five — which, to keep things in proportion, still puts it ahead of roughly eight
thousand others.
