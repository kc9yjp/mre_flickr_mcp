## The Critique

*Filed from a desk somewhere west of Lake Michigan.*

Five favorites. I'd like to file a formal complaint with the bureau.

This is the sleeper of the collection and — I'll say it plainly and you can hold me to
it — the best-made portrait of the five. Everything the lake picture wanted and couldn't
have, this one has. Directional light: that window is doing Vermeer's work along the left
side of her face, and the spot meter held it honest. An actual interior moment: chin on
knuckle, eyes gone somewhere out the window that is frankly none of our business. A
background that behaves: the robin's-egg wall, the gold picture frame, the nailhead trim
on the chair — the whole corner could have been set-dressed by a Dutch painter on a
municipal budget. And the "no filter" machine tag is the proudest line of metadata in this
whole photostream, because the light needed no help.

It was made on an iPod touch at a fifteenth of a second. Let that be the lesson nailed
over the darkroom door: the light does not know what camera you're holding.

Quibbles, because quibbling is the job: the crop pinches her arm at the left edge and
clips the picture frame up top; the belt buckle throws one hot little glare at the bottom;
there's grain in the shadows if you go hunting for it. Don't go hunting for it.

**Verdict:** The reason this earned five faves is the same reason it's good: it's quiet.
Quiet doesn't chart. Sunsets chart. But if I could hang exactly one portrait from this
five — and I've walked a few gallery floors between Des Moines and Door County — it's
this one, and it isn't close.
