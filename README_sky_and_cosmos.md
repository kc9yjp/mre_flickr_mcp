## The Critique

*Filed from a desk somewhere west of Lake Michigan.*

Three hundred six favorites — the people's champion of this entire photostream — and for
once the critic and the crowd can share a booth. This is the best-loved photograph on the
account and it's also one of the best. That alignment is rarer than it ought to be.

The move that makes it is positional: getting *under* the flowers. This is the rabbit's
view of a garden, the one nobody bothers to take because it involves kneeling in the dirt
in October. Down here the petals stop being white objects and become white *windows* —
the light doesn't bounce off them, it comes through them, and every vein shows like the
leading in stained glass. And then the masterstroke nobody consciously notices but
everybody feels: that mackerel sky. Thousands of small cloud-flecks, the same size and
the same white as the petals overhead — the sky doing an impression of the garden, the
garden doing an impression of the sky. That rhyme is the whole picture, and you can't
plan it; you can only be lying under the cosmos the morning it happens.

The rainbow flare in the lower right is a phone-optics artifact worn as jewelry — the
description owns it ("dreamy lens flare"), which is the right call. Hiding it would've
been vanity; owning it is style.

Quibbles: the two lead blooms are twins competing for the same role, and the frame might
be stronger if one of them clearly outranked the other. The evergreen mass in the upper
right hangs like a stage curtain that wandered into the shot. The bottom edge is a
scramble of stems and grass-blades. Technically there's nothing heroic here — 1/7937th of
a second at ISO 32 is broad daylight, any camera made this decade could do it. Which is
exactly the point: nothing about this picture is equipment. It's all seeing.

**Verdict:** The crowd put the right photo on top. A garden flower photographed the way
nobody photographs garden flowers, on a morning the sky decided to cooperate. If the
account has a signature image, this is it.
