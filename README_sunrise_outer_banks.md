## The Critique

*Filed from a desk somewhere west of Lake Michigan.*

Let's start with the number: 154 favorites. Nothing else in this photostream comes within a
country mile of it, and the crowd, for once, has it about right — though I'd wager they
faved it for the frosting and missed the cake.

The cake is the staircase. Every beach access from Kitty Hawk to Corolla has been
photographed at dawn ten thousand times, but this frame understands what it actually has:
a doorway. The A-frame of the railings makes a gate, the two slat fences lean away on
either side like ushers pulling a curtain open, and the sun sits square in the opening
like it bought a ticket. The eye climbs those half-buried steps whether you ask it to or
not. f/11, sharp front to back, was the right and honest call — this is a picture about
structure, and the structure holds like a barn beam. The sand drifted over the bottom
treads says "wind and time" better than any caption could.

Now the pushback. The processing wears that cooked, silvery, early-2010s HDR sheen in the
sky — it dates the frame the way shag carpet dates a rec room. The sun parked dead center
is the safe placement; a half-step left and it burns through the lattice of the railing,
which is the stranger, better window. And here's the detail the pretty colors try to hush
up: the sandbags stacked along the right fence. The Outer Banks are washing out to sea,
and this postcard is quietly propped up on the evidence. That tension — vacation glow
sitting on top of erosion control — is the truer picture, and the frame looks politely
past it.

**Verdict:** The photographer who gets up at 6:08 on vacation earned every one of those
faves. The one who makes the sandbags the subject next time earns a gallery show.
