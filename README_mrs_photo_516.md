## The Critique

*Filed from a desk somewhere west of Lake Michigan.*

Here is my favorite kind of photograph: the kind that has no business working. The camera
is an iPod touch — not even the good phone. The shutter is dragging along at 1/15th of a
second. The Salamander filter is ladling vignette over everything like gravy at a church
supper. The composition is upside down. And it is the bravest picture in this top five by
a comfortable margin.

It's shot standing directly over her while she lies on the striped rug, so her face
arrives inverted at the bottom of the frame, mid-laugh. That inversion is the whole trick.
It makes you tilt your head, work a little, and then it pays you off with the most
unguarded expression in the collection. This is also the only photograph of the five where
you can feel the photographer standing *in* the picture rather than hiding behind it — you
can practically hear whatever joke got told the second before the shutter went. Intimacy
like that has never once come off a tripod.

Yes, the filter dates it to 2013 as precisely as carbon: the rounded black border, the
cooked warmth, the whole Flickr-iOS-app era in one swipe. But run the test — strip the
filter off in your mind and check whether the picture underneath still works. It does.
The real cost is the crop, which takes her arms mid-gesture and spends the top half of
the frame on shirt.

**Verdict:** Fourteen favorites. The crowd rewards sunsets and misses the good stuff;
always has, always will. This one belongs on the family wall — not despite being a little
dumb and a little tender, but because of it.
