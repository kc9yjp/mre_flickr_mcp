## The Critique

*Filed from a desk somewhere west of Lake Michigan.*

This is a cloud portrait, and the cloud earned it. That great white plume in the upper
middle — read it as a wing, a swan, an angel losing altitude, whatever your upbringing
supplied — with a twisting column dropping out of it like a rope thrown down from the
loft. January skies over Chicagoland run to gray sheet metal nine days out of ten; on the
tenth day they produce *this*, and the whole neighborhood looks up from its shoveling for
about ninety seconds. The picture's job was to be outside, pointed correctly, during the
ninety seconds. Job done.

The smart decision is what the trees are allowed to be: black lace, nothing more. Bare
winter canopy as filigree framing, left and right, no pretense that they're the subject.
Meanwhile the frame runs a two-act structure — the gold-and-orange stratus band burning
low on the left is the *actual* sunset, and the white plume up top is the show the sunset
is lighting. Fire below, feathers above.

Quibbles: the right-hand tree mass is a gray thicket that eats a third of the frame and
never quite resolves to true silhouette — it muddies where the left trees crisp. Power
lines saw across the lower left; authentic urban texture by one reading, clutter by
another, and on the tenth look I lean clutter. The composition never fully picks between
its two acts — the eye ping-pongs from gold band to white plume without a bridge. And
somewhere in the edit, the EXIF got wiped — the camera's testimony stripped out, which a
records-minded critic files under misdemeanor.

**Verdict:** Cloud pictures are a lottery you can't practice for; you can only keep a
camera in your pocket and keep looking up. 233 faves says the neighborhood sky drew a
winning number, and the photographer had the sense to cash the ticket.
