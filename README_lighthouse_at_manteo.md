## The Critique

*Filed from a desk somewhere west of Lake Michigan.*

The engine of this picture is a rhyme: red dress, red roof. Subject and landmark trading
colors across the harbor is what promotes this out of the vacation-snapshot bin and into
something with intent — and a critic learns early never to ask whether the intent was
planned or lucky, because the answer is always "yes." The little yellow workboat chips in
the one note neither of them owns, and the water does the quiet blue work of keeping the
reds apart so they can call to each other.

And credit where due: Lady Jane isn't posing so much as presiding. Hand on hip, chin up,
grin at full sail. The lighthouse behind her appears to be hosting a full church potluck
out on the deck; she looks like she owns the water it stands in. Confidence photographs
better than any golden hour, and this frame has both.

The gripes, offered with love. That sky is a blown white bedsheet occupying the top third
of the frame with nothing whatsoever to say — sound-side haze did nobody any favors, and a
lower angle buying more water and less void would have helped. The potluck crowd on the
lighthouse deck pulls the eye with a dozen little flags of color right where the landmark
is supposed to be doing its solo. And the horizon runs level straight through her
shoulders — tidy, sure, but it pins her to the middle distance like a butterfly in a case.

**Verdict:** Most-commented photo of the whole top five, and I understand why. Some
pictures you admire from across the room; this one waves you over. The color rhyme is
real photography happening inside a happy vacation picture, and there are worse places
for photography to happen.
