## The Critique

*Filed from a desk somewhere west of Lake Michigan.*

Call it the rematch. Eleven years after the dune-stairs sunrise took 154 faves and the
top spot of its era, the same photographer walked the same town's sand at 6:27 in the
morning and made a better picture, and the audience — 289 faves — agreed. It's worth
saying why this one wins.

The 2013 photo was scenery: an invitation, a doorway, a postcard with good bones. This
one has a *subject*. The wave, caught mid-collapse across the full width of the frame,
its spray backlit at the crest — the ocean actually doing something, photographed at the
moment of doing it. The 80mm telephoto is the right knife: it compresses the shorebreak
against the horizon so the violence and the calm sit stacked in shallow layers, and it
keeps the photographer's feet dry, which I respect. And the color story is a
two-temperature picture done properly — foam running cool blue-gray in the shadow of its
own curl, sky running peach and rust above it. Two little birds hang in the left sky
like commas, spacing the sentence.

Quibbles: the sun's disc has gone slightly molten — that bean-shaped bloom phone HDR
makes when a sun refuses to stay round. The foreground foam field is a heavy gray apron
that asks more of the bottom third than it repays. And the haze band flattens the middle
distance into a wash — atmosphere giveth mood, atmosphere taketh depth.

**Verdict:** The 2013 sunrise invited you to walk toward the sea; this one reminds you
the sea doesn't care whether you come. That's growth. Eleven years of standing on
beaches at dawn will teach a photographer what the good hour is actually for.
